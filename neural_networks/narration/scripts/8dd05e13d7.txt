The pipeline worked great, but it needed us to know the problem's structure in advance. Could a single network figure out the shape by itself? Let's try the smallest one possible: just two neurons in a chain. One squashes the input through sigmoid, and the other scales and shifts the result. Four weights total.

First, generalize your gradient descent loop into a function called fit_net that works on a list of parameters — nudge each one, compute its gradient, then step all of them. Then define the error function for this two-neuron model and train it. The result will be better than a straight line, but not perfect — because sigmoid flattens out where the tax keeps climbing.
