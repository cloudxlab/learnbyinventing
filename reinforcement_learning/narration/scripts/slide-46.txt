Before we dive into gradients, let's pause on a quick question: why do we work with the log of the probability instead of the probability itself? Over a 200-step game, you're multiplying 200 numbers that are each less than one. That product shrinks to something so tiny your computer can't handle it. Taking the log turns that product into a sum, which stays a sensible size. It's the same information, just in a form that doesn't vanish.
