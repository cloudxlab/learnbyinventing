Before we dive into gradients, here's a quick recap on why we work with the log of the probability, not the probability itself. Over a long game, you're multiplying hundreds of numbers that are each less than one. That product shrinks toward zero so fast that your computer can't even keep track of it. Taking the log turns that product into a simple sum, which stays a perfectly reasonable size. It's the same information, just in a form that won't vanish on you. You'll put it to use in the next exercise.
