Our class matrix was small, just twenty-five people and fifteen movies. Now imagine Netflix: hundreds of millions of users and tens of thousands of titles. A user-similarity matrix that size is far too big for any single machine to even hold.

But two ideas rescue us. First, almost every rating is missing, so why store all those zeros? Keep only the real ratings. Second, matrix multiplication breaks into tiny independent products, and each one can run on a different machine. Then we just group and add them up by cell.

That's MapReduce.
