Here's a neat question: why does merge sort land on n times log n? Think of the recursion as a stack of levels. At every level, the total work across all the pieces adds up to about n comparisons.

So how many levels are there? Ask yourself how many times you can halve n before you're down to single items. You already invented that number back in Binary Search.

Now try the ratio yourself for a few values of n. Then compare it to the million-item case.
