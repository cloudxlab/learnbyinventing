The answer is B. Counting sort needs an array as large as the range of possible values. For ages 0 to 200, that's just 201 slots. But for arbitrary floating-point numbers, the range is effectively infinite. You'd need impossibly many buckets. That's why comparison-based sorts like merge sort remain essential for general-purpose sorting.
