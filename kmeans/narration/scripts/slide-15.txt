# Part 4: How Many Groups? K-Means needs you to choose K — the number of groups. But how do you know the right K? What if you pick too many? Too few? Let's find out by measuring how "good" each grouping is.
# ---
Now we're moving into a new question: how many groups should we ask for? K-Means makes us choose K ahead of time, and that's a real decision. Pick too few, and different kinds of points get squished together. Pick too many, and we split natural groups for no good reason. So let's invent a way to measure how good a grouping is.
