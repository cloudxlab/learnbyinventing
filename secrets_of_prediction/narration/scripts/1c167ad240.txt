# The dot product is affected by vector lengths. A vector twice as long gives a dot product twice as big, even if the direction is the same.
# [Fragment 0]: To measure direction only , take the dot product of the unit vectors . This gives a number between [math] and [math] called cosine similarity : [math] same direction [math] perpendicular [math] opposite
# ---
Divide out the lengths and you get a pure direction score. It's called cosine similarity, and it runs from negative one to one: one means same direction, zero means perpendicular, and negative one means exactly opposite.
