You just derived Ordinary Least Squares, the same closed-form answer a computer would give you. So why does the next chapter exist? Because this ancient method has two cracks. First, the cost of solving grows quickly as models get bigger, just like you estimated back in Part 2. Second, "set the slope to zero" only works if you have a formula for it, and complicated models don't offer one. Gradient Descent fixes both by walking downhill toward the minimum instead of jumping straight there. Ready to take that walk?
