Look at everything you just built, one step at a time. You started by noticing that plain differences cancel out, and then you fixed that two different ways: squaring and absolute value. You compared them, took their gradients, and ran into that sharp corner where absolute error isn't differentiable. Then you stitched two pieces together, spotted the jump, and solved for the constants that make it smooth.

That's the Huber loss. Statisticians published it in 1964, and you just rediscovered it from scratch.
