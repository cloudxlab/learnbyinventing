# Why this matters: Our training algorithm (gradient descent) works by computing the gradient of the loss and using it to update weights. If the gradient doesn't exist at some point, the algorithm breaks down. MSE ([math]): Differentiable everywhere. Gradient = [math] — smooth, well-defined, no problems. But the gradient grows without bound for large errors, making training unstable with outliers. MAE ([math]): Not differentiable at diff = 0. The sharp corner means gradient descent has no direction to follow when the prediction is exactly right. So MSE has a problem with large errors, and MAE has a problem at zero error. Can we do better?
# ---
Here's a thought. Gradient descent only works if the loss gives us a clear slope to follow at every point. MSE is smooth everywhere, but its gradient keeps growing as the error grows, so one big outlier can throw training off. MAE has the opposite problem: it has a sharp corner at zero error, so there's no clear direction right where the prediction is perfect. Each loss fails somewhere. Could we build one that gets the best of both?
