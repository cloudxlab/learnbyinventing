Here are a few ways to take this further. There's a smoother cousin of Huber that rounds off even the transition point. There's a version that treats over-predicting and under-predicting differently, for when one mistake costs you more than the other. And there's another smooth option that's twice differentiable everywhere.

But the best way to explore is to experiment. Take your Huber loss and try different values of delta. What happens as delta gets really tiny? What about when it gets huge? Watch how the curve changes, and see if you can explain why.
