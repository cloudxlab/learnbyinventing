# Why this matters: Our training algorithm (gradient descent) works by computing the gradient of the loss and using it to update weights. If the gradient doesn't exist at some point, the algorithm breaks down. MSE ([math]): Differentiable everywhere. Gradient = [math] — smooth, well-defined, no problems. But the gradient grows without bound for large errors, making training unstable with outliers. MAE ([math]): Not differentiable at diff = 0. # ---
Here's the catch with both of our loss functions. Our training method, gradient descent, depends on the slope of the loss, so it needs a slope to exist everywhere. Mean squared error is perfectly smooth, but when a prediction is way off, its slope explodes, and outliers can throw training off. Mean absolute error handles big mistakes calmly, but it has a sharp corner right at zero error, so there's no clear direction to follow there. Each one fixes the other's weakness. So can we do better? Think about it for a moment.
