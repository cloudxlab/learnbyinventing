Now we're switching gears. Imagine you're building a task scheduler, something like cron. Tasks show up in any order, each with a run time, and all the scheduler ever needs is the earliest one. Add a task, pop the earliest. That's it.

Before reading on, try rating each strategy yourself. How much work is adding or popping for an unsorted list, a sorted list, or a tree? The tree does well, but it keeps everything in perfect order, and we only ever ask for the minimum. Could something that promises less do the job with less work? It can. It's called a min-heap. Its one rule: every node is no bigger than its children. So where must the minimum be?
