# What you just invented -- the trade-off ladder you climbed: A dictionary promises the most (any-key O(1)) and pays in memory and lost ordering. A BST promises total order and pays log n per operation -- plus a balance headache (solved by AVL / Red-Black trees; B-Trees take the same idea to disk in every database). A heap promises the least -- just the minimum -- and is the simplest and fastest of all. Choosing a data structure = paying only for the promises you actually use. Bonus tricks: showed that recursion is just a stack you can manage yourself, and turned a string into an expression tree -- text in, -ready tree out.

You've just climbed a whole ladder, so let's look back at it. A dictionary promises the most: instant lookup by any key. It pays for that in memory and lost ordering. A BST promises order, and pays a little on every operation. A heap promises only the minimum, so it's the simplest and fastest. Choosing a data structure just means paying only for the promises you'll actually use.
