# Going further: O(2^n) exponential, O(n!) factorial, Amortized analysis, P vs NP
# ---
If you want to go further, here are some fascinating directions. Exponential algorithms, O of 2 to the n, try every possible subset. They work for tiny inputs but become impossible around n equals 100. Factorial algorithms, O of n factorial, are even worse. Amortized analysis handles operations that are usually fast but occasionally slow, like appending to a Python list that needs to resize. And P versus NP is the biggest open question in computer science: are there problems where checking a solution is easy but finding one is fundamentally hard? There's a million-dollar prize waiting for the answer.
