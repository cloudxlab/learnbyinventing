You just invented Big O notation. Computer scientists write it as a capital O with the growth family in parentheses, so an algorithm that grows like n squared is "big O of n squared." It goes back to Paul Bachmann in 1894, and Edmund Landau later popularized it. But the idea is exactly what you discovered: group algorithms by how they grow, and ignore the constants.
