Your deep tree scored a perfect zero error on its training data. That should make you suspicious, not proud, because it just memorized. So here's the big idea: train many trees and average them. Their mistakes point in different directions and cancel out, while the shared signal survives. It's the same reason a crowd's average guess of jellybeans in a jar beats almost every individual. But there's a catch. If every tree sees the same data, they're all identical, and averaging identical trees just gives you one tree. We need them to be different.
