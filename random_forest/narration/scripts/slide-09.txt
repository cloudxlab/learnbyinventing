# When Should the Tree Stop Asking Questions? Left alone, the tree splits until every leaf holds a single house --- that's not learning, it's memorization : perfect on training data, brittle on anything new. Two brakes prevent it: --- the maximum number of questions from root to leaf. A shallow tree is forced to generalize. --- the smallest group still allowed to be split. Splitting a 2-house group into two 1-house "groups" scores a perfect variance reduction, and learns nothing. There's nothing to compute here --- just convince yourself why each brake prevents memorization. You'll wire both into the tree next.
# ---
Here's a question worth asking: when should a tree stop? If we let it keep splitting, it'll keep going until every single house has its own leaf. That might sound thorough, but it isn't learning. It's memorization, and it falls apart the moment it sees a new house.

So we need two brakes. One limits how many questions the tree can ask in a row. The other says a group that's too small shouldn't be split at all. Nothing to code here. Just think about why each one protects us, then we'll build them in.
