Here's a twist: instead of a string like "20 + 40", the expression is stored as a nested list. The operator comes first, then the left side, then the right. So a plus with 20 and 40 gives you 60, and you can nest another list inside to build bigger expressions.

Before you code, think about the base case: what if you're handed just a plain number? Then there's nothing to solve, so you simply return it. Otherwise, solve the left and right parts recursively, then apply the operator. Give it a try!
