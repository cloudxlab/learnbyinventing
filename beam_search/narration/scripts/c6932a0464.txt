# One refinement using log-probabilities instead of raw probabilities. Multiplying many small probabilities leads to tiny numbers that can underflow. Adding log-probabilities is more stable:
# ---
Here's a refinement that real systems almost always use. When we score a candidate sequence, we've been multiplying probabilities together. But multiply enough small numbers and you get something tiny, so tiny that the computer can round it down to zero. That's called underflow. The fix is simple: work with log-probabilities, where we add instead of multiply. Let's see how that looks.
